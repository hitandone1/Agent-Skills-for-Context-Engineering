# Publication Production

<!-- coderabbit-ai-generated:v1 -->

## When to Activate

Use when drafting, revising, or preparing a paper, report, article, or other publication from evidence. Do not activate for source discovery and evidence assessment: use research-synthesis; or for individual visual assets: use asset-production.

<!-- /coderabbit-ai-generated:v1 -->

## Core Concepts

Publication readiness requires a coherent argument, traceable evidence, and a reproducible account of methods. A polished draft cannot make absent experiments or fabricated citations real.

## Practical Guidance

1. Identify audience, intended venue, requested format, existing material, authorship, and submission constraints. Infer reversible formatting choices and mark missing material explicitly.
2. Build an outline and claim-to-evidence map. Verify each citation against the source, and distinguish measured results from proposals or interpretation. Do not invent datasets, participants, results, approvals, or references.
3. Draft to the venue's requirements. Describe methods, data provenance, analysis, limitations, and reproducibility details when relevant. Keep uncertainty and negative results aligned with the evidence.
4. Reconcile figures and tables with source data and text; check captions, numbering, links, bibliography, and accessibility. Render the requested format and inspect the resulting file for layout errors.
5. Deliver the editable source and requested exports where possible, with unresolved evidence gaps and checks performed. Submission, publication, or contacting others requires the user's authorization.

## Examples

For a paper based on incomplete experiments, write the supported methods and clearly mark results as pending. For a supplied article, preserve its claims while checking citations and creating an accessible final export.

## Guidelines

- Preserve the author's intended claims and disclose substantive changes.
- Apply venue-specific templates and disclosure rules when supplied or verified.
- Clearly label drafts and distinguish reproducible analysis from a narrative summary.

## Gotchas

1. **Citation drift**: A citation may support a different result than the sentence claims; check the actual source.
2. **Polished fabrication**: A fluent results section can conceal missing measurements; leave unsupported results pending.
3. **Export regressions**: Broken references or clipped figures can appear only after rendering; inspect the final export.

## Integration

- research-synthesis owns research and source appraisal.
- asset-production owns figures and other reusable assets.
- evaluation supports checks of the underlying analysis or agent workflow.

## References

Use supplied evidence and the destination's current author instructions when available.
